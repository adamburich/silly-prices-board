Silly Prices Research Explorer

A record of what we tested, what survived, what failed, and how those results shaped the investment system.

Silly Prices is a long-term investment system built around a simple idea: identify unusually cheap, high-quality companies, then use context-aware analysis to distinguish temporary problems from permanent impairment.

This explorer documents the research behind that system — successful tests, failed hypotheses, unseen-data holdouts, null tests, implementation audits, and live monitoring. Negative results are treated as first-class evidence rather than hidden. Several of the most load-bearing findings here are things that did not work.

What we've learned

Screening improves the hunting ground

Mechanical screening identifies better opportunities than simply buying large price declines. Companies passing the funnel outperformed both vetoed names and naive large-drawdown purchases — this supports Stage 1 as a candidate generator, not as a complete investment decision.

CONCLUSION 3

Judgment is a necessary part of the system

Mechanical rules alone could not reliably identify the false bargains that caused the worst mistakes. Multiple economically plausible filters were tested: some were inert, some worked in only one period, and one performed worse than deleting the same number of signals at random.

Best evidence E1 Idiosyncratic filter · E5 Gate ablations · E9 Regime-adaptive · E11 Normalized operating earnings
CONCLUSION 4

Staged buying is primarily exposure control

The three-lot ladder controls how much capital one diagnosis can command. It is not evidence that averaging down creates return — the cap sweep found no per-lot return decay out to lot 8, which is precisely why the cap is set on risk grounds rather than found as a return optimum.

Best evidence B3 · B4 Ladder cap sweep · C9 Funding cadence · D1 Lot-size exposure · G4 Enforcement audit
CONCLUSION 5

Selling should be rare

Broad mechanical selling rules generally did more harm than good. Every policy containing mechanical thesis-break exits lost in every configuration tested. The retained valuation exit is deliberately rare — roughly 26–34 sells in 22 years — and as of 2026-08-15 it executes automatically. Thesis breaks do not: they route to judgment, because that is the one thing the evidence says machines cannot do here.

CONCLUSION 6

The largest remaining unknown is prospective judgment quality

Historical testing tells us how good the decision layer needs to be. It does not prove the live model achieves that skill, and nothing on this page does. The campaign established that in calm regimes the mechanical funnel runs at roughly market parity, so most of the claimed value has to come from Stage-2 judgment — which makes this the one measurement that could confirm or refute the system's central premise, and the one that cannot be hurried.

The machinery to answer it exists: decisions are logged in full and can be reconstructed from the record alone. The evidence itself does not — it requires running forward and waiting.

Theme 1 — Finding opportunities

Does the screening process find unusually good opportunities?

Before asking a language model to reason about a company, the system needs a useful candidate pool. These tests ask whether the mechanical screen identifies more promising situations than simpler alternatives.

C3 + C4 Point-in-time funnel, two universes · 2026-08-10

Does the screen sort winners from losers, or just find falling prices?

Across a survivorship-free universe, do companies that pass the funnel perform differently from vetoed companies and naive large-drawdown purchases?

✓Hypothesis Supported ◇Evidence Controlled · point-in-time ▲Production Adopted as Stage 1

The three cohorts separate monotonically at every horizon, and the ordering replicates inside a point-in-time S&P 500 membership universe. Passing the funnel beat the survivorship-free market; being vetoed by the trap sweep did materially worse; buying a 30% decline with no gates at all did worst of all.

Why it matters This is the evidence that Stage 1 earns its place — and the evidence for its limit. The funnel improves the hunting ground; it does not follow that every passing company should be bought. That gap is exactly the job Stage 2 exists to do.
Guardrail on this claim The supported claim is candidate enrichment / discrimination. This is not evidence that "Stage 1 beats the market" or that the funnel is a profitable autonomous strategy. The campaign synthesis is explicit that in dislocation-poor regimes the mechanical funnel averages roughly market parity.
Limitations
  • One 28-year path; no Stage-2 judgment is modelled anywhere in these simulations.
  • Point-in-time fundamentals begin 1998 — 1995 is not reachable at retail (C0).
  • Delisted names are frozen at last trade; alphabetical rationing in cash-constrained weeks.

E1Idiosyncratic filter· 2026-08-11 · branch mechanized

Is a decline more attractive when it is the company's own, not the sector's?

The live system requires a name's decline to exceed its sector benchmark's by 10 percentage points. Is that rule worth anything?

✕Hypothesis Rejected ◇Evidence Random-deletion null ▲Production Not adopted in harness

Rejected. The filter performed worse than removing the same number of signals at random — losing to the random null by 4.2 percentage points over 1998–2019 and 3.8 points over 2000–2019, both outside the seed ranges.

This one is running with real money The report's own status line calls it "the most damaging thing measured in this campaign, and it is running with real money." One mitigating distinction is recorded and not glossed: the harness models this as a hard veto; the live system applies it as a demotion — a sector-wide name still appears in the screen table labelled no (sector-wide), excluded from the zone list rather than deleted. Live's version is therefore less severe than what was measured.
The falsification that did not fire This test was also set up to falsify the project's central claim. If requiring idiosyncratic dislocation had erased the edge, much of what is called "selection" would really be "bought beta at the bottom." It didn't: the edge survives arbitrary thinning almost intact (−2.1% and +0.0% under random removal), so it does not depend on any particular subset. That is a genuine defence of the central claim — and the report notes it was the outcome the author would have bet against.
Limitations
  • Three random seeds — a range of three draws, not a confidence interval.
  • The harness runs point-in-time S&P 500; live runs a curated watchlist with real sector ETFs.
  • Sharadar sector labels are current, not point-in-time — affects the benchmark, not the strategy.
  • The (unclassified) sector has no members, so unclassified names pass unconditionally — a small leak toward under-filtering.

E2 + E3Quality bar and capital scale· 2026-08-11

Does a stricter quality bar help — or does the answer depend on how much money you have?

What is the Piotroski F-score gate worth, and why did the answer disagree across time windows?

◐Hypothesis Mixed → resolved ◇Evidence Controlled deconfounding ▲Production F ≥ 7 retained

Two of three windows are cleanly monotone against loosening the bar — every point off the bar costs money. The third disagreed, and disagreed hard. E3 resolved it: the disagreement is capital, not calendar.

Why it matters This is the clearest case in the project of a result that looked like a calendar effect and turned out to be an exposure effect. It also surfaced a live-vs-harness divergence nobody had noticed: the harness enforces F ≥ 7 as a hard gate; the live system does not enforce it at all, vetoing only on the trap flag F ≤ 3. Live's effective bar is 4.

  • Live's lot is fixed at tranche_usd = 1000 rather than scaling with contributions, so lot-proportional scale-invariance does not apply to it.
  • --open-with is a diagnostic, not a strategy knob, and is not part of the frozen configuration.

Why it matters — this result outranks everything else in the campaign The implicit procedure of the entire campaign — pick the best backtest cell — failed the only honest test run of it. Freezing the configuration before looking at the reserved period

How do you stop later work from retroactively claiming an untouched holdout after seeing it?

◈Evidence Provenance · pre-declared ▲Production Adopted as method

D5 fixed the rule: develop on 1998–2019, reserve 2020–present, because crisis-free holdouts have little power for this system. E12 wrote the exact configuration down before the reserve run — and the ordering is the point. E13 is the single evaluation of that frozen configuration on the reserved period.

Why the ordering is load-bearing E12's own opening states it: if the configuration is only recorded afterwards, there is no way to distinguish what was frozen from what was chosen having seen the result. The file also records, parameter by parameter, whether each value was chosen on numbers or on class logic — only three (trim line 0.97, global F bar 7, mega-cap F bar 5) were chosen on numbers, and all three were re-measured on scores corrected for a split double-adjustment.
Why it matters This is the project's procedural answer to its own worst result. The holdout showed point optima do not transfer; the freeze is the change that stops the next cycle from quietly re-fitting them.
Limitations
  • E13's result is recorded inside the frozen-config and mega-cap-reserve artifacts rather than as a standalone report.
  • Work done after the reserve run (E15) is contaminated by having seen it, and is badged as such rather than hidden.

Theme 3 — Temporary problems vs false bargains

Can rules distinguish a temporary problem from a value trap?

A company can be cheap for a good reason. These experiments tested whether simple mechanical signals could reliably identify the candidates that looked attractive but later produced poor outcomes. This section is why Stage 2 exists.

The division of labour, stated by the evidence The campaign synthesis puts it in one line: machines may enter (the gates discriminate) and may rarely trim (it is safe); machines may never sell on deterioration (it is procyclical). Stage-2 judgment is the layer that has to beat the trap sweep's crisis false-positives — and since the mechanical funnel is roughly at parity in calm regimes, Stage-2 non-degradation is the whole game in calm markets, and crisis judgment is the whole upside.
E11 · E10Normalized operating earnings· 2026-08-11 · branch normalized-ebit

Could normalized earnings repair the cyclical false-bargain problem?

Does normalizing operating earnings improve the buy side enough to justify continuing the research programme?

■Hypothesis Stopped ◈Evidence Pre-registered stop condition ▲Production Not built

The Phase-1 stop condition fired. Four independent solvency preconditions were designed — through credit-analyst, reuse-existing-gates, earnings-quality and minimal-parameters lenses — each validated against the database and each attacked by a separate adversary hunting verified counterexamples. When lot size is held fixed, what does capping the number of purchases actually do?

Deconfound the earlier sweep: hold lot size fixed so the cap only truncates the sequence of lower-price fills, and every cap is a prefix of the same fills on identical episodes.

◐Hypothesis Mixed — cap is a risk dial ◇Evidence Controlled, matched-lump benchmark ▲Production Cap 3 adopted on risk grounds

Return on deployed capital rises monotonically with the cap, and there is no per-lot return decay out to lot 8. That is precisely why the cap is not a return optimum: the evidence gives no return-maximising stopping point, so the cap has to be set on risk instead.

A designed metric that failed — and the report says so The matched-lump column is tautological. An only-lower ladder's every fill after the first is below the first by construction, so it beats same-dollars-at-first-purchase whenever two or more lots fill. The constant 85 / 86% across caps is just the share of episodes with a second fill. It confirms the schedule dominates its own matched lump, but it cannot discriminate between caps — which was the thing it was built to do.
Why cap 3, then The cap is a risk-budget dial, not a return parameter. In-sample, anything from 3 to 6 is defensible; higher caps buy more median profit at a near-linear cost in measurable tail risk. Uncapped is ruled out — fill counts reach 20+ in long grinds, so uncapped at $1k lots means the worst grinds take $18–24k in a single name, with position size largest exactly where the decline lasted longest. The principled sizing is top-down: cap × lot size = the most you are willing to lose in one name when the diagnosis is wrong.
Guardrail Allowed: the ladder controls deployment and exposure. Not supported by this evidence: that averaging down creates return. The decision record is explicit that staging costs the median episode in every regime tested, on every metric — that cost is an insurance premium, accepted deliberately.

B1 · B2 · B3 · B5How the ladder replaced three tranches· 2026-08-09

Why staged entry at all — and why this shape of it?

Could a simpler schedule beat the earlier three-tranche approach or a matched lump-sum purchase?

✓Hypothesis Supported — as insurance, not alpha ◇Evidence Controlled + external literature ▲Production Adopted 2026-08-09

The old three-tranche design wasn't staging at all: all three tranches filled by week 9 against bottoms at week 20–25, and the third fired before the second. Its +10% bounce trigger was a false signal about 60% of the time in every regime. The replacement — buy one lot per week while rated BUY, only at a price strictly below the last fill, never more than three per episode — beat a lump sum in 70–77% of episodes, the best of nine structures tested.

The reframing that mattered The literature review (B2) found no credible raw-return edge for staging anywhere. Constantinides condemns predetermined schedules and endorses information-conditioned ones; Hayley warns never to judge these by internal rate of return. So the ladder is judged as misdiagnosis insurance — on dispersion and worst case — not as a return generator. Every surviving mechanism conditions on information: a lower offered price plus a clean sweep against the newest filing. Price action is never a trigger.
What the weekly re-check buys Discrete tranches validated the thesis only at their trigger moments. The ladder revalidates the entire funnel — zone, sweep, verdict — before every dollar. One rule replaced four trigger legs, two of which were unbuildable in practice.
Limitations
  • B1 is price-only, so it can reach 2000–02 and 2008–09 that point-in-time fundamentals could not — at the cost of modelling no fundamentals.
  • B2 is external literature, not a backtest result.
  • Staging costs the median episode in every regime tested; the protection is in the tail.

Theme 8 — Live prospective validation

Is the live decision system actually working?

Historical backtests can only tell us what would have been required in the past. The decisive test of the judgment layer is prospective: freeze the evidence and the decision when it happens, then evaluate the outcome later. That evaluation is not yet available — what exists today is the machinery to make it possible, and the operational monitors below.
