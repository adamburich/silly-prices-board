Profile B. The same total capital as Profile A for each window, all of it deposited at week 0, no further injections. Lots are capital ÷ 1200 — roughly 100 concurrent names at the 12-lot episode cap — so sizing scales with the money, as it does in the live rule.

An earlier version of this tab held the lot at a flat $200 to isolate timing. That made a $400k investor deploy in 0.05% increments, leaving ~89% of the capital parked in the growth reserve through the 2000–02 collapse, and the result said more about the sizing artifact than about lump-sum funding. The ratio is the honest control; the flat dollar was not.

The ÷1200 divisor is a judgement call, not a derived optimum. On the 2000 window alone, terminal value runs from $1.2M at a $200 lot to $8.0M at $10,000 — this parameter matters more than the reserve tilt does, and no single window should be allowed to set it.

Compare B to A within a window. Do not compare B rows to each other. Each window’s seed matches that window’s total contributions in Profile A, so the seed varies with window length — $580k for 1998–2026 down to $140k for 2020–2026. A longer window therefore starts with more capital, which is a confound across rows here that does not exist in Profile A. The A-vs-B comparison for any single window is clean: identical total capital, identical $200 lot, only the arrival schedule differs.

It shows up directly in the starved column. 2000–2026 starves nothing while 2000–2019 starves 1,198 — same start, same strategy, and the only difference is that the longer window was seeded with $140k more.

Reading the numbers

Every window is in-sample. They measure fit, not skill — and point optima do not transfer here: the campaign measured a development-to-holdout Spearman ρ of 0.24.

The edge is crisis-concentrated, which is what the replay shows and a terminal value hides.

Gross of everything — no commissions, slippage, taxes or market impact. Total return throughout (Sharadar closeadj); plain close drives signals only.
